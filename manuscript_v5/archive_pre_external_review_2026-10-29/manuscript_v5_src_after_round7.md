# Coverage, agreement and provenance of candidate platinum-response labels in TCGA-OV

Aaron Michael Anuroop Chintala¹*, Christopher Lockhart¹

Short title: Platinum-response labels in TCGA-OV

¹ School of Systems Biology, George Mason University, Manassas, VA, USA

\* Corresponding author: michaelanuroop@gmail.com (AMAC). ORCID (AMAC): 0009-0009-0062-6453

## Abstract

Machine-learning studies that predict response to platinum-based chemotherapy from The Cancer Genome Atlas ovarian cancer cohort (TCGA-OV) define "response" in different ways, often without stating the definition. In 489 platinum-treated patients we compared TCGA's published platinum status, a platinum-free interval re-derived from the TCGA Clinical Data Resource, first-course response and, as candidate survival surrogates taken from our own earlier analyses, death before 18 or 24 months (overall-survival, OS, thresholds). Platinum status was available for 277 patients (57%). The re-derived interval agreed closely with it where both were available (κ 0.97); a patient-level audit of the 117 patients it added classed 78 as unflagged, and a provenance-filtered candidate extension that adds only those covered 355 patients (99 resistant), although it added only 2 resistant patients with RNA-seq data. At 18 and 24 months the OS thresholds flagged 21% and 39% of platinum-resistant patients; of flagged patients with a known status, 82% and 74% were resistant, against 31–32% expected without platinum information, but about half of flagged patients had no status. No threshold between 6 and 60 months agreed better than moderately with platinum status (highest κ 0.47, at 36 months). *BRCA1/2* variants and *CCNE1* amplification pointed the same way under every label, so these markers do not resolve the comparison. Expression classifiers trained on four of the labels predicted them modestly (AUC 0.61–0.68); trained in the same 134 patients with the same folds, platinum-trained and 24-month-OS-trained models did not differ detectably in predicting platinum status (difference in AUC −0.005, conditional 95% interval −0.105 to +0.087; out-of-bag refits of the whole pipeline ranged from −0.18 to +0.16). We release a versioned patient-level label table with the provenance of every re-derived call. Studies should state label definitions and class sizes, prefer platinum-specific labels, document provenance, and treat OS thresholds as survival end-points.

## Introduction

High-grade serous ovarian cancer (HGSOC) is usually diagnosed at an advanced stage and treated with cytoreductive surgery and platinum-based chemotherapy [@tcga2011]. Most patients respond initially, but many relapse with platinum-resistant disease, and no pre-treatment genomic test of platinum response is in routine use. The TCGA-OV cohort [@tcga2011] has therefore been used repeatedly to study expression-based prediction of platinum response (Table 1). A meta-analysis of machine-learning models for platinum response in ovarian cancer reported pooled concordance indices of 0.81 in training and 0.83 in validation cohorts [@wangq], and an earlier systematic review of chemoresistance prediction found that overall survival, progression-free survival and response to chemotherapy were all used as end-points, and that most studies predicted survival or prognosis rather than response to treatment [@lloyd].

A classifier is trained to predict whatever its label records. Clinically, platinum response has been judged by the time since the last platinum dose, which predicts response to platinum re-treatment [@markman], with a six-month cut-off conventionally used to define resistance, as in TCGA's own platinum status [@tcga2011]; the Gynecologic Cancer InterGroup has since replaced this dichotomy with the therapy-free interval for trial design [@wilson17], but a binary classifier, such as those studied here, still needs a dichotomous label. In TCGA-OV several candidate labels are available: the platinum status published with the 2011 data, a platinum-free interval that can be re-derived from the TCGA Clinical Data Resource (TCGA-CDR) [@liu], the response recorded after first-line therapy, and, as candidate surrogates, thresholds on overall survival (OS), the most complete outcome in the cohort. Composite groups that combine a survival window with platinum resistance have also been used, for example short-term survivors defined by overall survival of 6 months to 2 years, primary platinum resistance and progression within six months of completing platinum [@yangsyc], and a review of 44 expression studies found 12 different definitions of platinum resistance [@talijanovic]. None of the TCGA-OV studies in our non-systematic review used an OS threshold (Table 1); we evaluate the 18- and 24-month thresholds because our own earlier classifier of this cohort used them [@chintala2026], not because they are established practice. Reporting guidelines for prediction models require the outcome definition to be reported, and risk-of-bias tools treat it as a distinct source of bias [@tripod; @probast]; in the meta-analysis above, only 4 of 39 platinum-response models were rated at low risk of bias in the outcome domain [@wangq]. In measurement terms, each label is an operationalisation of an unobserved construct, and whether it measures that construct is separate from how well a model predicts it [@jacobs].

Earlier work re-annotated TCGA-OV treatment outcomes and related platinum sensitivity and first-line response to overall survival [@villalobos]; most closely, Cocchi et al. compared platinum-free-interval categories derived from that re-annotation with the original TCGA annotation and reported that variability in patient selection, platinum-sensitivity definitions, data sources and analytical pipelines limits the reproducibility of the genes reported [@cocchi]; the TCGA-CDR considers the progression-free interval generally the more informative end-point in pan-cancer analyses while recommending all four of its end-points, including OS, for TCGA-OV [@liu], OS has been described as a noisy outcome label because it includes deaths from non-cancer causes [@yousefi], and label choice affects drug-response prediction in cell lines [@bayer]. In the literature we reviewed, however, no study had quantified agreement across the families of candidate labels in TCGA-OV — platinum-based, survival-based and response-based — within the same patients, evaluated OS thresholds as tests for platinum resistance, compared which outcomes classifiers trained on each label predict, or released a patient-level label table with the provenance of each call.

We therefore asked: for training a platinum-response classifier on TCGA-OV, how do the candidate labels differ (i) in how many patients they cover, which patients they call resistant and, for the re-derived interval, which records support each call, (ii) in their associations with characteristics linked to platinum response and to prognosis, and (iii) in what expression classifiers trained on them predict? Our aim is not to evaluate any individual study.

## Materials and methods

### Data and cohort

This is a secondary analysis of publicly available data. Clinical, treatment, mutation, copy-number and expression data for TCGA-OV were obtained from cBioPortal [@cerami; @gaoj] (PanCancer Atlas 2018 release, study `ov_tcga_pan_can_atlas_2018`, and the TCGA 2011 release, `ov_tcga_pub`) and from the TCGA-CDR [@liu]. Of 585 patients in the PanCancer Atlas clinical file, 496 had a treatment-timeline record, and 489 had carboplatin or cisplatin recorded as the agent; these 489 form the cohort. The 89 patients without any treatment record were excluded because their treatment could not be established; their median overall survival was 27.6 months, against 47.7 months in the cohort. Expression values were RNA-Seq by Expectation-Maximization (RSEM) estimates from the PanCancer Atlas release; 251 platinum-treated patients had RNA-seq data. Cohort characteristics are given in S4 Table. The study used only publicly available, de-identified data released by TCGA under its consent and governance procedures; no further ethics approval or consent was required.

### Labels

Five labels were constructed for the 489 patients, each coded 1 for non-response or resistance and 0 otherwise, together with two combinations of the platinum labels (Table 2).

*Platinum status.* TCGA's published platinum status [@tcga2011], taken unchanged from the `ov_tcga_pub` release. The TCGA definition measures the platinum-free interval from the last primary platinum dose and states that "Platinum status was defined as resistant if the platinum free interval was less than six months and the patient had progressed or recurred" [@tcga2011, Supplementary Methods S1]; sensitive tumours had an interval of six months or more, and patients without progression who had been followed for less than six months after the last dose were excluded. Patients in the release's "Tooearly" category, with missing status, or absent from the 2011 release were treated as undetermined.

*Platinum-free interval from first-line platinum.* A re-derivation from different source fields: the TCGA-CDR progression-free interval time [@liu] minus the stop date of the last platinum regimen recorded with an adjuvant indication, both measured in days from diagnosis. Platinum rows were treatment-timeline rows whose agent name contains "platin" (carboplatin and cisplatin, and oxaliplatin, which supplies the anchor for 3 patients, 2 of them with a determinate interval); no platinum row carried a neoadjuvant indication. Restricting the anchor to carboplatin and cisplatin changed no call and left 2 patients undetermined, 1 of them among the unflagged added patients (analysis A23). When several adjuvant platinum rows were recorded (60 of the 389 patients with a determinate interval, 8 of them with overlapping rows and 7 with tied stop dates), the latest stop date was used; 1 patient had an adjuvant platinum row without a stop date, 1 had an adjuvant row starting after a later-line regimen, 6 had a platinum row with another or no indication, and 23 cohort patients had no adjuvant platinum row. With alternative anchors — the earliest adjuvant stop date, the end of the first platinum regimen whatever its indication (which also labels 18 patients without an adjuvant row), or the latest stop date among adjuvant rows starting before any later-line regimen (which leaves one patient undetermined) — 0 to 5 of the calls determinate under both anchors changed (κ with the primary label about 0.97; S1 Text, analysis A18); none of the 78 unflagged added patients changed under any of them. Patients with progression less than 183 days after the end of platinum were labelled resistant and patients with an interval of 183 days or more sensitive; patients censored before 183 days were undetermined. Patients whose recorded progression preceded the end of platinum (a negative interval) were classed resistant (n = 8); those censored before the end of platinum were undetermined (n = 11). TCGA-CDR counts death with tumour as a progression event and dates progression by new-tumour-event records [@liu]. A broad combined label used platinum status where it was determinate and this interval otherwise. We then audited the provenance of every re-derived call (S1 Text, analysis A15; added after the main results were known). Each resistant call was traced to a recorded new tumour event (recurrence or progression) or to a death counted as progression. Each patient was flagged for a redacted TCGA-CDR record, an adjuvant platinum regimen without a stop date, a later-line regimen started after the end of platinum but before the recorded event (for sensitive patients, within 183 days of the end of platinum), or an interval within 150–215 days of the end of platinum. Resistant patients whose event was a recorded new tumour event, and sensitive patients, were classed unflagged if they had no flag; resistant calls dated only by a death counted as progression were never classed unflagged, and negative intervals formed a separate class (unflagged patients are labelled clean_resistant or clean_sensitive in the label table). The 150–215-day flag was specified before the audit was run, to mark calls that a dating error of about a month could reverse; it does not imply that such records are wrong, and the label without it (367 patients; label table column label_combined_strict_asrun, with its OS-threshold test characteristics in S1 Text) is reported as a sensitivity analysis. A provenance-filtered combined label used platinum status where it was determinate and the re-derived interval only in unflagged patients (it is called the strict combined label in the analysis summaries in S1 Text, and label_combined_strict in the label table). Published platinum status was kept as the reference wherever it was determinate and only the re-derived additions were filtered, so the combined label does not apply uniform quality requirements: it is a conservative extension of platinum status, and the accuracy of the added calls is not verified. By a rule fixed before the audit was run, the provenance-filtered label became the preferred of the two combined labels, and the broad label a sensitivity analysis, because more than 20% of the added patients could not be classed unflagged; it is a candidate extension, not a label shown to be more accurate. The provenance-filtered label is used in the label table and in the evaluation of OS thresholds against a combined reference; the other analyses use platinum status, the re-derived interval or the broad combined label, as stated.

*First-course response.* Stable or progressive disease versus complete or partial response after primary therapy, taken from the TCGA-CDR treatment-outcome field, then from the `ov_tcga_pub` release, then from the PanCancer Atlas timeline; this order was fixed after a survey of the three sources, which agreed in 365 of 365 and 141 of 142 shared patients.

*OS thresholds.* Death before 18 or 24 months from diagnosis versus survival beyond the threshold, with patients censored before the threshold excluded. No study in Table 1 used an OS threshold. The 18- and 24-month thresholds are those used in our own earlier classifier of this cohort, which defined platinum response as survival beyond 12, 18 or 24 months [@chintala2026]; the present study supersedes that interpretation, and we evaluate them as candidate survival surrogates for platinum resistance, not as established practice; a 12-month threshold is reported in the Supporting information, and all thresholds from 6 to 60 months were examined in the agreement analysis.

### Coverage and agreement

For each label we counted the platinum-treated patients in whom it was determinate. Patients with and without a determinate platinum status were compared on overall survival (Kaplan–Meier medians, log-rank test), death before 18 and 24 months, the re-derived interval and first-course response (Fisher's exact test). For each pair of labels, agreement was measured in patients with both labels determinate by Cohen's κ [@cohen], with 95% confidence intervals from 2,000 bootstrap resamples of patients, together with the maximum κ attainable given the pair's marginal frequencies. The 18- and 24-month OS thresholds were evaluated as tests for platinum resistance, with platinum status as the reference: sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV) and negative predictive value, with Wilson 95% intervals [@wilson27]. After internal review we added, as exploratory analyses, the same evaluation at every threshold from 6 to 60 months; comparison with the values expected if the OS label carried no information about platinum status (PPV equal to the prevalence of resistance; specificity equal to the proportion of patients the label does not flag) and with the highest sensitivity the label's prevalence allows; and bounds on sensitivity and PPV obtained by assigning all OS-labelled patients without a status to resistant or to sensitive. Because patients censored before a threshold have no OS label, the same measures were re-estimated with inverse-probability-of-censoring weights (Kaplan–Meier, and a Cox model of censoring on age, stage IV and residual disease) [@vock] (S1 Text, analysis A19). The censoring distribution was also estimated only in patients with a platinum status, and separately within each status group (analysis A23); the largest weight was 1.24, and no weights were truncated.

### Associations with characteristics linked to platinum response and prognosis

We asked whether the labels differ in their associations with characteristics known to be associated with platinum response. Germline and somatic *BRCA1/2* variants are associated with platinum response and with survival in ovarian carcinoma [@pennington; @yang], and *CCNE1* amplification with primary treatment failure [@etemad]. Carriers were patients among the 272 sequenced platinum-treated patients in the 2011 release with a germline or somatic truncating or splice-site *BRCA1* or *BRCA2* variant (55 carriers, 44 with a germline variant); *CCNE1* amplification was a GISTIC call of 2 in the same release. Each label was also tested against characteristics linked to prognosis: residual disease after surgery (more than 1 cm versus less, as defined by TCGA [@tcga2011], and, as an alternative coding, any versus none), stage IV disease, Aneuploidy Score, tumour mutational burden (TMB) and age. Binary characteristics were tested by Fisher's exact test with conditional odds ratios (OR) for non-response, and continuous ones by the Mann–Whitney U test with rank-biserial correlation (r). For the five pre-specified clinical and tumour characteristics, P values were adjusted by the Benjamini–Hochberg method [@bh] within each label. To compare labels directly, the patients with platinum status and both OS thresholds determinate were analysed together; differences between labels in the log OR for *BRCA1/2* (platinum label minus OS label) and for residual disease and in r for aneuploidy (OS label minus platinum status) were estimated with 95% intervals from 2,000 bootstrap resamples; 0.5 was added to every cell of each table (Haldane correction), so no resample was discarded. The smallest difference detectable with 80% power was derived approximately from each interval in an exploratory analysis; it describes sensitivity, not power.

### Classifiers

Logistic regression (L2 penalty), random forest (300 trees) and a radial-basis-function support vector machine were fitted, each with balanced class weights and fixed random seeds, in scikit-learn 1.9.0 [@sklearn]. Expression was log2(RSEM + 1)-transformed and restricted to the 15,994 genes also measured on the external platform described in the Supporting information; the 500 genes with the highest variance and the standardisation parameters were computed on training data only. For four training labels (platinum status, platinum-free interval, OS at 18 and 24 months), models were trained and evaluated by five-fold stratified cross-validation repeated five times over the 251 patients with RNA-seq data. Each model scored all held-out patients, and pooled out-of-fold scores were evaluated against every label, giving a train-label by test-label matrix of AUCs. We report the mean AUC over three models and five repeats and its range; these AUCs describe which outcomes the classifiers predict, not what biological information they use. This all-patients analysis compares labels on different patient sets and class balances and is reported as descriptive; its pre-specified decision rule is reported in S1 Text. To compare training labels directly (analysis A16, added after the main results were known), the same pipeline was trained with platinum status, OS at 18 months and OS at 24 months in the 134 patients with RNA-seq data in whom all three labels were determinate, using the same outer folds for every label (five-fold cross-validation stratified on platinum status, repeated 20 times). The primary quantity was the difference in AUC for platinum status between platinum-trained and 24-month-OS-trained models, averaged over the three models; each model's difference was computed from its own predicted probabilities, which were never pooled across models. Its 95% interval came from 2,000 bootstrap resamples of patients, stratified by the target label, using out-of-fold scores averaged over repeats; models were not refitted within the bootstrap. The same script rerun in a second software environment gave the same conclusions, which checks computational consistency only (S1 Text). As a sensitivity analysis of the whole procedure, the pipeline (gene filter, scaling and the three models for each training label) was also refitted in 500 bootstrap samples of the 134 patients, drawn within platinum-status groups with duplicates kept, and every model and label was evaluated on the same patients not drawn (analysis A22; scikit-learn 1.7.2, in which random-forest fits differ slightly from those in the A16 environment). All 500 samples gave a valid AUC for every contrast, and none had a single-class training label or out-of-bag target. SVM probabilities came from scikit-learn's internal five-fold Platt scaling. Because single fits on about 63% of the distinct patients, evaluated on the remainder, estimate a different quantity from the repeated cross-validation, the out-of-bag results are reported separately, with their own median and percentile range; the analysis's pre-specified rule, which made this range the main uncertainty, was therefore not followed (S1 Text).

### Exploratory analyses

Three analyses were added after the main results had been seen, at the request of internal reviewers, and are reported as exploratory. First, overall survival was divided into the platinum-free interval and survival after progression (among patients who progressed), and univariable Cox models of each were fitted for age, stage, residual disease, *BRCA1/2*, *CCNE1* and Aneuploidy Score. The platinum-free interval was measured from the end of first-line platinum, and only the 408 patients at risk at that time (interval above 0 days) were included; the 28 patients with an interval of 0 days or less were excluded (analysis A13b): the 19 negative intervals described under Labels (8 with an event, 11 censored) and 9 intervals of 0 days (4 with an event, 5 censored). Of the 489 patients, 53 had no re-derived interval (52 without a recorded stop date for first-line platinum and 1 without a TCGA-CDR progression-free interval time), so 436 entered this step and 408 were analysed; each model's sample size and number of events, after missing covariate values, are given in S10 Table. A model that sets those intervals to 1 day is reported as a sensitivity analysis (S10 Table). Times were TCGA-CDR days from diagnosis (PFI.time, OS.time) and treatment-timeline stop dates. A rank-based test of proportional hazards gave P < 0.05 in 2 of the 14 models (age for the platinum-free interval, Aneuploidy Score for survival after progression; those two constant hazard ratios should be read with caution because proportional hazards was not supported), and an added quadratic term did not detect non-linearity for either continuous covariate (P ≥ 0.28; S1 Text, analysis A20). Survival after progression was overall survival minus the TCGA-CDR progression-free interval, so patients whose progression event was their death were excluded from that model. The two models were not compared formally. Second, a logistic regression on age, stage IV and residual disease (> 1 cm), with five-fold cross-validation repeated five times, was used to ask how far each label can be predicted from clinical characteristics alone. Third, the re-derived interval was recomputed without the 38 patients whose TCGA-CDR record is marked as redacted. The threshold sweep, reference bounds, minimum detectable differences and an alternative coding of residual disease (any versus none) were added at the same time. The clinical-only models used logistic regression only.

### Supplementary analyses

Three further analyses are reported in the Supporting information: transfer of the TCGA-trained classifiers to an independent microarray cohort, GSE63885 [@lisowska]; a gene-set transfer experiment, in which the 10 genes of Chen et al. [@chen] and the 5 genes of Gao et al. [@gao] used as fixed inputs to our pipeline and compared with 1,000 random gene sets of the same size (both sets were selected in data that included TCGA-OV patients, which favours them in TCGA, and the experiment neither replicates nor tests the original models); and the AUC inflation produced by selecting genes on the full dataset before splitting, a well-documented error [@ambroise; @vabalas; @teschendorff; @whalen], measured with OS-threshold labels and the pipeline's own simpler preprocessing (untransformed RSEM values for all 20,531 genes). GSE63885 (Affymetrix GPL570; series-matrix log2 RMA values; probes mapped with hgu133plus2.db, keeping the probe with the highest mean expression per gene; genes z-scored within GSE63885, using that cohort's own expression distribution, so this is a batch-level transfer assessment rather than a frozen pipeline applied to single new patients) was restricted to serous and undifferentiated tumours (80 samples). Its platinum-resistance label (disease-free survival < 180 days; 34 resistant, 40 sensitive) and clinical-response label (stable or progressive disease versus complete or partial response; 10 of 74) are defined verbatim, with sample identifiers, in S7 Table; differences in transfer performance cannot be attributed to the training label alone. The original publication analysed 97 tumours, 72 of them serous or undifferentiated with clinical data (62 responders, 10 non-responders; 33 platinum-resistant) [@lisowska]; the deposited series has 101 samples, and our 74 annotated serous or undifferentiated samples include 2 more responders and 1 more resistant tumour, which cannot be identified because the publication lists no sample identifiers. Chen et al. reported 75 samples (34 resistant, 41 sensitive), consistent with including one endometrioid tumour excluded here.

### Literature review

For TCGA-OV studies of expression-based platinum-response prediction identified in an initial literature search and an additional targeted search (neither systematic; databases, dates and search terms were not recorded, so the selection cannot be reproduced and "none" below means none in this sample) [@gonzalez; @sunj; @choi; @gb2021; @sunx; @guoli; @chen; @li; @wangc24; @gao; @kilim; @wangc], we recorded from the full text and supplements the label used, its stated definition and source, the number of patients in each class, the validation design and the reported discrimination. Items not reported were recorded as "not stated"; no definition was inferred.

### Pre-specification

Where an analysis had predictions or a decision rule, they were written into its script header before it was run (the time strings of two headers, A18 and A21, were corrected after their runs, and a bug in A22 was fixed before it produced any result; all are disclosed in S1 Text; the decision rule of A22 was not followed, for the reason given under Classifiers). The scripts for the analyses of *BRCA1/2* and *CCNE1*, of patients without a platinum status, of prognostic characteristics in the full cohort, of provenance and of the controlled classifier comparison were deposited in the public code repository before they were run (commits d5bd844, e98b3d9 and d349b2b); the other headers are archived with the code but were not timestamped externally. Three levels of specification apply: predictions for the initial label analyses (A1–A7), written before those analyses but after the authors' earlier work on the same cohort; secondary predictions written after earlier results of this study had been seen (A8–A10, A15, A16), which may have been informed by them; post hoc analyses with decision rules (A13b, A18, A19, A21, A23, and A22 with one prediction); and exploratory or descriptive analyses without predictions (including A20). No hypothesis was fixed before any inspection of TCGA-OV data, and commit timestamps establish only the order of writing and running. The descriptive full-cohort tables and the analyses labelled exploratory were added after the main results were known. One material deviation occurred: the provenance-audit code omitted one pre-specified flag (an interval within 150–215 days), and the reported label comes from a correction run that adds only that flag (S1 Text). All predictions and their outcomes, including those that failed, and the development history of the analyses are given in S1 Text. All scikit-learn analyses were run in one pinned environment (Python 3.14.5, scikit-learn 1.9.0, NumPy 2.4.6), except the exploratory clinical-only models, the fold recount in A20 and the full-pipeline bootstrap in A22 (Python 3.10, scikit-learn 1.7.2); Cox models used R 4.6.0 (survival 3.8.6) and, for the exploratory analyses, lifelines 0.30.0. Reporting follows the STROBE statement [@strobe], and STARD 2015 for the evaluation of OS thresholds as tests for platinum resistance [@stard] (S2 Text).

## Results

### Studies label patients in different ways

Twelve studies of expression and platinum response that used TCGA-OV, or a cohort used alongside it, applied at least five label constructions, and none used an overall-survival threshold (Table 1). Five labelled patients by progression or recurrence within six months of the end of platinum or chemotherapy, but differed in the reference point and in whether intermediate patients were excluded [@gonzalez; @choi; @gb2021; @guoli; @chen]; one compared platinum-refractory disease with no progression within two years [@kilim]; one used progression-free survival as a surrogate [@sunx]; and two used class counts identical to those of TCGA's published platinum status (90 resistant, 197 sensitive) without stating a definition [@li; @wangc24]. Four did not fully state how their TCGA patients were labelled [@sunj; @li; @wangc24; @gao]. In this illustrative sample, the proportion of TCGA patients labelled resistant ranged from 19% to 44% (S1 Table).

### Platinum status covers just over half of treated patients

TCGA's platinum status was determinate for 277 of the 489 platinum-treated patients (57%; 87 resistant, 190 sensitive), and for 145 of the 251 with RNA-seq data (Fig 1A, Table 2). The remaining 212 were recorded as missing (87), too early to classify (57) or were absent from the 2011 release (68). Among patients in the 2011 release, those without a determinate status did not differ in overall survival from those with one (median 47.5 versus 47.7 months; log-rank p = 0.37), but the group was mixed: patients with missing status had a median survival of 39.0 months, whereas only 10 of 57 patients classed as too early had died. Patients without a status were more often dead before 18 months (16 of 96 versus 22 of 268; OR 2.23, 95% CI 1.04–4.70) and, less clearly, before 24 months (24 of 95 versus 43 of 259; OR 1.70, 0.92–3.09), and more often had stable or progressive disease after first-line therapy (28 of 114 versus 28 of 255; OR 2.63, 1.41–4.91), but were less often resistant by the re-derived interval (13 of 68 versus 89 of 272; OR 0.49, 0.23–0.96). The re-derived interval was determinate for 389 patients (358 without the redacted TCGA-CDR records; κ with platinum status 0.964 versus 0.966), and the broad combined label for 394 (113 resistant). The OS thresholds were determinate for 419 patients at 18 months (47 non-responders) and 401 at 24 months (78 non-responders).

Of the 389 patients with a re-derived interval, 272 also had platinum status and 117 were added by the re-derivation (26 resistant). Of the 26 added resistant calls, 20 were dated by a recorded new tumour event (13 recurrences, 3 progressions and 4 of unrecorded type) and 6 by a death counted as progression. Of the 117 added patients, 78 were unflagged (12 resistant, 66 sensitive) and 39 were not. Counting each patient once, 1 had a negative interval, 6 had a resistant call dated only by a death counted as progression, 20 had another record problem (a redacted TCGA-CDR record, adjuvant platinum without a stop date, or a later-line regimen before the event), and 12 were flagged only for an interval within 150–215 days (Fig 1D, S11 Table); these 12 are exactly the patients that the label without that flag adds. In all four patients in whom the two platinum labels disagreed, the re-derived interval called resistant a patient TCGA had called sensitive. One of the four had an interval near the cut-off (154 days); the other three (18, 101 and 148 days) had a recorded recurrence and no flag, and one of these (101 days, three adjuvant platinum rows) became sensitive under two of the three alternative anchors, which themselves create other disagreements; the remaining two are not explained by the available records (S11 Table). Two of the 12 unflagged resistant additions rest on a new tumour event of unrecorded type; "unflagged" means that no specified flag applied, not that the call was verified. Across all 115 resistant re-derived calls, 90 were dated by a recorded recurrence, 15 by a recorded progression, 4 by a new tumour event of unrecorded type and 6 by a death counted as progression. Setting aside the 10 calls dated only by a death or by an event of unrecorded type (none of whom had a platinum status, so κ with platinum status was unaffected) changed the sensitivity and PPV of the OS thresholds against the broad combined reference by at most 0.04, and changed the *BRCA1/2* and *CCNE1* odds ratios by at most 0.05, with no interval moving across 1 (S1 Text, analysis A21; S11 Table). The provenance-filtered combined label was determinate for 355 patients (99 resistant, 256 sensitive; Fig 1A) and is the preferred of the two combined labels under the pre-specified rule; without the 150–215-day flag it would cover 367 (104 resistant). Its practical gain for expression classifiers is small: among patients with RNA-seq data it adds 22 patients to platinum status, only 2 of them resistant (145 to 167 patients; 41 to 43 resistant). Against it, the OS thresholds had sensitivities of 0.23 at 18 months and 0.41 at 24 months and PPVs of 0.79 and 0.75, within 0.05 of their values against the broad combined label (S1 Text).

### OS thresholds agree at best moderately with platinum status

Where both were available, the published and re-derived platinum labels agreed in 268 of 272 patients (κ 0.97, 95% CI 0.93–0.99; Fig 1B). Agreement of the OS thresholds with platinum status was low: κ 0.24 (0.14–0.35) at 18 months and 0.37 (0.25–0.49) at 24 months, against maximum attainable values of 0.33 and 0.59. At 18 months the OS threshold flagged 18 of 84 resistant patients (sensitivity 0.21, 0.14–0.31) and 4 of 184 sensitive patients (specificity 0.98); at 24 months it flagged 32 of 83 resistant (0.39, 0.29–0.49) and 11 of 176 sensitive patients (specificity 0.94; Fig 1C). These values are largely set by how few patients die early: with no information about platinum status, the same labels would have specificities of 0.92 and 0.83, and their prevalence allows sensitivities of at most 0.26 and 0.52. The PPV is informative alongside these values: of the flagged patients with a determinate status, 18 of 22 (0.82, 0.61–0.93) and 32 of 43 (0.74, 0.60–0.85) were resistant, against 0.31 and 0.32 expected without platinum information. However, 25 of the 47 patients flagged at 18 months and 35 of the 78 flagged at 24 months had no platinum status; if all of them were sensitive or all resistant, the PPV would lie between 0.38 and 0.91 at 18 months and between 0.41 and 0.86 at 24 months. These values also exclude patients censored before each threshold (8 and 17 of those with a platinum status; 69 and 87 in the cohort, in which 1 further patient has no recorded survival time). With inverse-probability-of-censoring weights, sensitivity, specificity and PPV changed by at most 0.04 (S1 Text). The Kaplan–Meier weights assume censoring unrelated to outcome, and the Cox weights (which excluded 23 patients with missing covariates) unrelated given age, stage and residual disease; estimating the censoring distribution only in patients with a status, or within each status group, changed the estimates by at most 0.01. Weighting does not address missing platinum status. Across thresholds from 6 to 60 months (exploratory), sensitivity rose and specificity and PPV fell, and agreement was at best moderate: κ was highest at 36 months (0.47; sensitivity 0.64, specificity 0.83, PPV 0.65; S2 Table). The two OS thresholds agreed with each other (κ 0.71), and first-course response agreed weakly with every other label (κ 0.21–0.26).

### Characteristics linked to platinum response point the same way under every label

*BRCA1/2* carriers were less often labelled non-responders under every label, although the evidence was imprecise for first-course response (OR 0.64, 0.18–1.80; Fig 2A, Table 3): 7 of 37 carriers versus 54 of 149 non-carriers by platinum status (OR 0.41, 95% CI 0.14–1.04), 8 of 47 versus 64 of 179 by the re-derived interval (0.37, 0.14–0.87), 1 of 51 versus 23 of 195 by OS at 18 months (0.15, 0.004–0.97) and 3 of 50 versus 43 of 192 by OS at 24 months (0.22, 0.04–0.75). In the 180 patients with platinum status and both OS thresholds determinate, the difference in log OR between platinum status and the OS thresholds was +1.12 (95% CI −0.16 to +2.00) at 18 months and +0.95 (−0.52 to +2.71) at 24 months; the approximate minimum detectable ratios of odds ratios were about 4.7 and 10, so these comparisons do not show that the labels behave alike (S3 Table). *CCNE1* amplification was associated with non-response in the same direction under every label (OR 1.11–2.01; Fig 2B).

Residual disease and aneuploidy behaved similarly (Fig 2C and 2D, Table 3). With residual disease coded as more than 1 cm, it was associated with non-response under both OS thresholds (OR 2.86, 1.30–6.29, and 2.41, 1.28–4.50) and first-course response (2.55, 1.31–4.91), but not under platinum status (1.12, 0.59–2.09); with any residual disease versus none, it was associated with platinum status (3.22, 1.40–8.35), the re-derived interval (3.04, 1.44–7.03) and OS at 24 months (2.78, 1.12–8.29). Aneuploidy Score was higher in non-responders under both OS thresholds (r = 0.20 and 0.25) and the re-derived interval (r = 0.21), and weakly under platinum status (r = 0.13, q = 0.26). In the same patients, none of the contrasts between platinum status and the OS thresholds could be distinguished from zero (difference in r for aneuploidy −0.06, −0.35 to +0.21, at 18 months and +0.11, −0.09 to +0.31, at 24 months; difference in log OR for residual disease > 1 cm +0.37, −0.66 to +1.25, and +0.50, −0.23 to +1.21), and again only large differences were detectable. Older age was associated with both OS thresholds (r = 0.26 and 0.28) but not with platinum status (r = 0.07), as predicted in advance for the 24-month threshold in patients with RNA-seq data (S1 Text). Full results, including raw P values, are in S5 Table. First-course response showed no clear evidence of association with *BRCA1/2* (OR 0.64, 0.18–1.80) or with aneuploidy (r = −0.03). These markers therefore do not resolve the comparison between labels.

### Exploratory: associations with the platinum-free interval and with survival after progression

When overall survival was divided into the platinum-free interval (408 patients at risk at the end of platinum) and survival after progression (S10 Table), *BRCA1/2* carriers had a longer platinum-free interval (hazard ratio 0.55, 95% CI 0.38–0.79), and *CCNE1* amplification (1.58, 1.19–2.09) and higher Aneuploidy Score (1.23 per SD, 1.10–1.38) a shorter one, with weaker or no associations with survival after progression (Fig 4). Age and residual disease > 1 cm were associated with shorter survival after progression (1.26 per 10 years, 1.12–1.42; and 1.41, 1.03–1.93) but not clearly with the platinum-free interval (1.09, 0.98–1.20; and 1.08, 0.82–1.41). Setting the excluded intervals to 1 day instead changed no hazard ratio by more than about 0.03 and no conclusion. Any residual disease was associated with both. A clearer association in one model than in the other is not evidence that the associations differ. Separately, a logistic regression on age, stage and residual disease predicted death before 18 and 24 months (cross-validated AUC 0.64 and 0.62) but not platinum status (0.48), the re-derived interval (0.52) or the broad combined label (0.49).

### Classifiers trained on each label predict modestly

Within TCGA, expression classifiers predicted each label modestly (Fig 3A): mean cross-validated AUC was 0.63 for platinum status (range over models and repeats 0.56–0.69; S6 Table), 0.61 for the re-derived interval, 0.63 for OS at 18 months and 0.68 for OS at 24 months. Models trained on the OS thresholds predicted platinum status with AUCs of 0.55 and 0.60. These all-patients comparisons use different patients and class balances for each label and are descriptive. In the controlled comparison (134 patients, 39 platinum-resistant, the same folds for every label; Fig 3B), platinum-trained and 24-month-OS-trained models did not differ detectably in predicting platinum status (difference in AUC −0.005; conditional 95% interval −0.105 to +0.087). The corresponding difference for 18-month-OS-trained models, which had only 7 positives (3 to 7 per training fit, and none in 22 of the 100 held-out folds), so that this comparison is particularly unstable, was +0.080 (−0.045 to +0.201); on the 24-month OS label, 24-month-OS-trained models scored no better than platinum-trained ones (difference −0.069, −0.236 to +0.081). The estimate is the AUC difference for out-of-fold scores averaged over the 20 repeats (the mean of the per-repeat differences was −0.016), and the conditional interval reflects the sampling of patients but not the refitting of models. In the out-of-bag sensitivity analysis, the median differences were +0.01 (2.5th–97.5th percentiles −0.18 to +0.16), +0.06 (−0.15 to +0.24) and −0.004 (−0.23 to +0.22) for the three contrasts (Fig 3B); each replicate is a single fit evaluated on 49 patients on average (with as few as one positive for the 24-month OS label), so this range describes the variability of single refits, not a confidence interval for the estimates above. No clear difference between training labels was demonstrated (S1 Text). Models trained on platinum status predicted OS at 24 months with an AUC of 0.63. On GSE63885, models predicted its platinum-resistance label weakly whichever label they were trained on (mean AUC 0.56–0.63); on its clinical-response label, which has 10 non-responders, models trained on the platinum labels had AUCs of 0.67 and 0.71 and OS-trained models 0.49 and 0.52; this comparison was not pre-specified, has no interval and is descriptive only (S1 Fig, S7 Table). In the gene-set transfer experiment, the 10 genes of Chen et al. separated platinum-resistant from sensitive TCGA patients better than random gene sets (AUC 0.735) but not GSE63885 patients (0.499), and the 5 genes of Gao et al. did not differ from random sets (S8 Table). Selecting genes on the full dataset before splitting raised mean AUC at the 18- and 24-month thresholds by +0.21 with supervised selection and by +0.006 with unsupervised selection (S2 Fig, S9 Table).

## Discussion

The candidate platinum-response labels in TCGA-OV differ in coverage, in which patients they call resistant, and in what they measure. The two platinum labels agree almost perfectly. Platinum status covers just over half of treated patients (277), the re-derived interval 389, and the combined labels about four-fifths (broad) or nearly three-quarters (provenance-filtered). The OS thresholds cover more patients, but at no examined cut-off between 6 and 60 months do they agree better than moderately with platinum status. At 18 and 24 months they flag an early-failure group: most flagged patients with a known status are platinum-resistant, but most resistant patients are not flagged, because platinum-resistant disease is compatible with survival beyond two years, and half of the flagged patients have no platinum status to check against. At longer thresholds they catch more resistant patients at the cost of flagging sensitive ones.

The exploratory decomposition, added after the main results were known, is consistent with this picture but does not establish it. For patients who progress after the end of first-line platinum, overall survival comprises the time to the end of platinum, the platinum-free interval and survival after progression. *BRCA1/2*, *CCNE1* and aneuploidy were associated more clearly with the platinum-free interval, and age and bulky residual disease more clearly with survival after progression, but the two models were not compared formally, and a clearer association in one model is not evidence of a difference. Clinical characteristics alone predicted the OS labels but not platinum status. An OS-threshold label may mix early platinum failure with other prognostic information; the decomposition raises this possibility but does not test it, and it would need confirmation in another cohort. Markers of platinum response cannot settle which label is better: *BRCA1/2* and *CCNE1* are associated with survival as well as with platinum response [@pennington; @yang; @etemad], so they point the same way under every label, and our same-patient comparisons could detect only large differences. With somatic variants alone, the OS labels showed no clear *BRCA1/2* association, because only 12 carriers had an OS label; including germline variants, which account for most *BRCA1/2* alterations in HGSOC [@tcga2011] and for 44 of the 55 carriers here, made the association visible under every label. First-course response behaved differently from all other labels — it agreed weakly with each of them and showed no clear evidence of a *BRCA1/2* association — consistent with a measure of response to surgery and chemotherapy together.

Within TCGA, expression predicted each training label with cross-validated AUCs near 0.6–0.7. When the labels were trained in the same patients with the same folds, no difference was detected between platinum-trained and 24-month-OS-trained models, so this study does not show that the choice of training label changes classifier performance; in those 134 patients (39 resistant), the conditional interval spans −0.105 to +0.087 and single refits of the whole pipeline varied more widely, so moderate differences in either direction cannot be excluded. With a standard, untuned pipeline and 41 resistant patients in the all-patients analysis, these estimates are imprecise and describe what this pipeline achieves rather than an upper bound for the data. They are nonetheless well below the figures reported in several studies [@wangq; @chen; @li; @gao], although those used other cohorts, data types and designs; selecting genes before splitting, one documented route to higher figures [@ambroise; @vabalas; @teschendorff; @whalen], inflated AUC by about 0.2 in our pipeline, which shows that it can inflate such figures but not that it explains any particular published result.

These results add to earlier work on TCGA-OV outcomes. Villalobos et al. re-annotated the cohort's treatment outcomes into platinum-sensitive, -resistant and -refractory groups (212, 91 and 28 patients) and found median overall survival of 27.0 months in patients whose treatment failed 1 to 6 months after the last platinum dose and 11.6 months in refractory patients [@villalobos]; our resistant category includes both, and the longer of these medians fits our finding that many resistant patients survive beyond the usual OS thresholds; Cocchi et al. reported that the genes identified by TCGA-OV studies vary with analytical pipelines and cohort definitions [@cocchi]. Cocchi et al. also released the patient barcodes and platinum status of their three cohorts (230, 201 and 142 patients) with their workflows. Our label table differs from both in its sources (the TCGA-CDR progression-free interval and the PanCancer Atlas treatment timeline), in covering all 489 platinum-treated patients, in recording the provenance and flags of each re-derived call, and in placing platinum, survival and response labels side by side in the same patients; it does not supersede their annotations. Patients without a platinum status are a mixed group — some died early or never responded, others were simply followed too briefly — so the missing values are unlikely to be random; re-deriving the interval from TCGA-CDR recovers about half of them (117 of 212), and where both labels are available they agree closely, which shows consistency with the published reference rather than accuracy in the patients without it. Two-thirds of the added calls (78) rest on unflagged records, and the provenance-filtered combined label keeps only those.

Several recommendations follow for studies of platinum response in TCGA-OV. State the label, its definition, its source field and the number of patients in each class. Prefer a platinum-specific label. If TCGA's platinum status is extended with the platinum-free interval re-derived from TCGA-CDR, document the provenance of each added call and report results with and without the added patients; the provenance-filtered candidate extension we release (S11 Table) is one such option, not a label shown to be more accurate. This is a conservative extension: the accuracy of the added calls is not verified, and the filter also removes borderline patients whose records may be correct. If an OS threshold is used, report it as a survival end-point rather than a platinum-response label, and expect low sensitivity for platinum resistance at short thresholds and poor specificity at long ones. Do not relabel first-course response as platinum resistance. Confine every supervised data-dependent step, including gene selection, to training data, and report any normalisation done within an external cohort.

### Limitations

The label analyses use one resource (TCGA-OV), with GSE63885 used only for external transfer, and platinum status is available for only 145 patients with RNA-seq data (41 resistant; the provenance-filtered combined label for 167, 43 resistant), which limits the precision of every classifier result. The same-patient comparisons of labels were underpowered, and their wide intervals do not establish that the labels are equivalent; the controlled classifier comparison likewise detected no difference but cannot exclude a moderate one in either direction; its interval is conditional on the fitted models, and the out-of-bag refits, which vary more, estimate a different quantity. Platinum status depends on the completeness of treatment and follow-up records, the re-derived interval shares TCGA's progression dates and counts death with tumour as progression, and their close agreement shows consistency rather than independent truth. The provenance audit checks the records behind each re-derived call, not the clinical events themselves, and at least two disagreements with unflagged records remain unexplained. The 150–215-day flag removes borderline patients whose records may be correct, which may make the filtered population less representative. Sensitivity and PPV of the OS thresholds are conditional on patients having a platinum status, and the bounds we report are wide. The cohort definition excludes 89 patients without a treatment record, whose survival was poorer than the cohort's (median 27.6 months) and only five of whom had a platinum status; the finding that patients without a platinum status did not have shorter survival holds only within the defined cohort. Both platinum labels use the six-month dichotomy that clinical consensus has replaced, for trial design, with a continuous therapy-free interval [@wilson17], and neither separates platinum-refractory from platinum-resistant disease. Excluding patients censored before an OS threshold can bias threshold labels [@vock]; a weighted analysis changed the estimates little, but it rests on assumptions about censoring that cannot be checked. *BRCA1/2* carriers were limited to truncating and splice-site variants called in the 2011 release; missense variants of uncertain significance and homologous-recombination-deficiency scores were not assessed. The decomposition of overall survival, the clinical-only models, the threshold sweep and the reference bounds were exploratory and were added after the main results were known. The decomposition describes patients who were event-free and under observation at the end of first-line platinum, not the whole platinum-treated cohort. Several pre-specified predictions failed, including that *BRCA1/2* would show no clear association with the OS thresholds and that platinum-trained classifiers would predict platinum status better than OS-trained ones; all predictions and outcomes are listed in S1 Text. TCGA expression data were processed centrally before any split we made, so batch effects introduced upstream cannot be controlled by our pipeline.

### Conclusion

In TCGA-OV, platinum-specific labels are consistent but cover only part of the cohort, and the re-derived interval extends them with records that can be checked patient by patient. Overall-survival thresholds, evaluated here as candidate surrogates, cover more of the cohort but agree at best moderately with platinum status. Expression predicts each of the four training labels modestly, and in the same patients the training label made no detectable difference to classifier performance. Which label a classifier is trained on determines how many patients it learns from and what it is asked to find; stating that choice, with class sizes, is a precondition for comparing models of platinum response built on these data. The versioned patient-level label table released with this paper (S11 Table; Zenodo, [[ZENODO_DOI]]) gives each patient's labels and the provenance of each re-derived call.
